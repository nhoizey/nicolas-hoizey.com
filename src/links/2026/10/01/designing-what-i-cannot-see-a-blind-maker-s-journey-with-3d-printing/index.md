> I found 3D printing in 2023, at a workshop run by IBOS, the Danish Institute for the Blind and Partially Sighted. I expected a room full of other blind people. I was the only one. It turned out the workshop was for sighted people, teaching them how to make things for blind people.
> 
> That was when I got hooked. Suddenly I could feel what a building looked like. Braille could be printed for almost nothing. Emergency maps, which hang on almost every wall as flat pictures, could be made into something I could actually read. If sighted people could learn to make these things for us, I could learn to make them myself.
> 
> That is why I started Accessible3D. It has two goals. The first is to make objects that blind and visually impaired people can actually use and enjoy: things with braille, with texture, with shapes that carry meaning. The second is to <mark>show sighted designers that accessibility is not an extra feature you bolt on at the end. It is a design choice, and it makes objects better for everyone.</mark>
